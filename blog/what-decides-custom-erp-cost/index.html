What Decides the Cost of Custom ERP Software?

Custom ERP has no fixed price list. These eight things decide what yours will cost, and here is how to keep the cost down without getting a weaker system.

"How much does an ERP cost?" is the first question every owner asks, and it is a fair one. The honest answer is that a custom system has no price list, because no two businesses need the same system.

What I can give you is better than a number pulled from the air: the eight things that decide the cost, and how to control each one.

1. The number of screens

Every form and every report is work. A system for one department might need ten screens. The ERP I built for a textile mill has 45 screens across eight modules.

How to control it: list the screens you will use every day. Build those first. Leave the "nice to have" screens for later.

2. How different the screens are from each other

Forty screens that all follow one pattern cost far less than twenty that each work in their own way. In the mill's ERP, most data-entry screens share one form pattern, one table and one report layout. A new report is mostly a query and a list of columns.

How to control it: accept a consistent look. It is cheaper to build and easier for staff to learn.

3. Users and roles

Ten people who all see everything is simple. Twenty roles in seven departments, each seeing only their own screens, is more work, and it is work worth paying for.

4. Your old data

Starting empty is cheap. Bringing years of records from Access or Excel is real work, because old data is never clean. I explained why in my Access to MySQL migration story.

How to control it: decide how many years of history you truly need.

5. Reports

Owners often expect reports to be the small part. They are not. Each report needs its own logic, totals, filters and print layout, and people check its numbers closely.

How to control it: name the three reports you read every day and the five you read every month. Start there.

6. Integrations

Email and WhatsApp alerts, barcode scanners, weighing scales, online shops, payment gateways and accounting software all add work. Each one also depends on another company's rules.

7. Where it runs

A system on your own server inside the building has almost no monthly cost, but needs a reliable machine and a backup plan. A cloud system costs money every month and needs a good internet connection.

8. Support after launch

A system is never finished, because your business keeps changing. Agree at the start how changes and support will be paid for, so there is no surprise later.

How to keep the cost down

  1. Start with one department. Choose the place where the pain is worst.
  2. Build in stages. Pay for a stage, use it, then decide on the next one.
  3. Reuse what already works. Keep the names and numbers your staff already know.
  4. Write down your process first. An hour with a pen saves days of rebuilding.
  5. Decide who answers questions. A developer who waits a week for every answer costs you a week every time.

What a good quote looks like

Be careful with any quote that is one number on one line. A quote you can trust shows:

  • the modules and screens included
  • the reports included
  • whether old data will be moved, and how much of it
  • what is not included
  • how stages are delivered and paid for
  • what support covers after launch

Cheap can be expensive

The most expensive system is the one your staff refuse to use. If people go back to paper and Excel after three months, every rupee spent was wasted, whatever the price. Fit matters more than price.
